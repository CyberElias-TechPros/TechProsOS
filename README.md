# **The ideal web application for TechPros.com.ng**

My strongest recommendation is to turn **TechPros into a professional IT operations and business technology management platform**, rather than another generic IT company website or a collection of unrelated online tools.

Think of a platform that combines the best ideas behind IT service management (ITSM), remote IT support, infrastructure monitoring, business automation, asset management, and technical reporting.

The central idea:

**TechPros should be a place where businesses can manage, monitor, request, document, and improve their technology operations.**

It should serve two audiences:

1. **Businesses and organisations:** People who need reliable IT services, technical support, infrastructure management, and business technology solutions.
2. **IT professionals and service providers:** People who need tools to manage clients, devices, support requests, technical projects, and service delivery.

This gives TechPros a much stronger identity: not just a company that provides IT services, but a technology platform that helps businesses operate better.

# **TechPros Operations Hub**

Recommended flagship product

A unified IT management, support and business technology operations platform.

**Suggested product identity:** TechPros One

**Tagline:** Your Technology. Under Control.

Other possible names:

- TechPros OS
- TechPros Command
- TechPros Nexus
- TechPros 360
- TechPros Control

I would make this a multi-tenant SaaS application. That means different businesses can register and operate independently, while TechPros manages the overall platform.

For example:

- A school manages its computers, printers, network and technical support requests.
- A hospital manages its IT assets, support tickets, network incidents and maintenance.
- A corporate office manages employees, devices, software, IT requests and vendors.
- An IT service provider manages multiple client organisations from one account.
- TechPros administrators oversee all customers, service delivery, subscriptions and platform performance.

This is much more commercially interesting than building a single-purpose ticketing application.

There is also a good foundation for this direction in established IT service management practices: service desk, IT asset management, service management, network support and enterprise application services are all recognised IT service categories.&#x20;

**Multi-tenancy is non-negotiable.**

## **Executive product decision**

TechPros should become a commercial software platform that enables organisations to manage their technology environments while enabling IT service providers to deliver and manage services for multiple customers.

It should not be limited to helpdesk software, network monitoring, asset management or project management.

It should bring these capabilities together through one connected system.

**The fundamental product proposition:**

> TechPros One gives businesses visibility and control over their technology operations, while giving IT professionals the tools to manage, support and improve those environments.

The platform will have two equally important operating environments:

1. **Organisation Workspace:** For companies managing their own IT operations.
2. **Service Provider Workspace:** For IT companies managing IT operations on behalf of multiple clients.

Both use the same underlying platform, but their workflows, permissions, dashboards and commercial models differ.

### **Strategic objectives**

| Objective         | Intended outcome                                 |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Centralisation    | One source of truth for IT operations            |
| Visibility        | Know what equipment, systems and services exist  |
| Accountability    | Know who is responsible for every issue          |
| Efficiency        | Reduce manual IT administration                  |
| Reliability       | Identify and manage infrastructure problems      |
| Documentation     | Preserve institutional technical knowledge       |
| Reporting         | Give management reliable operational information |
| Commercialisation | Create recurring SaaS revenue                    |
| Extensibility     | Support new industries, modules and integrations |

The product should be built around one principle:

**Every operational activity should produce a traceable record.**

A support ticket should be linked to its affected asset, location, technician, incident, resolution and reporting history wherever applicable.

That interconnected data model is what makes TechPros more valuable than a collection of independent tools.

## **1. Product architecture and overall structure**

I recommend a modular monolith architecture with clearly separated business domains, shared services and well-defined APIs.

This is preferable to starting with dozens of independent microservices. It gives us simpler development, easier deployment, lower initial operating costs and a clean path to separating services when justified by actual demand.

### **1.3 The five architectural layers**

| Layer             | Responsibility                                                        |
| ----------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |

| Application layer | Business modules and operational workflows                            |

# **2. Complete product module specification**

I recommend organising TechPros into 15 major modules.

The additional modules beyond basic ITSM are important because the earlier TechPros reporting platform concept included branches, facilities, finance, energy, procurement, licences, calendars and operational analytics.

## **Module 01 — Organisation & Workspace Management**

Platform foundation

This module manages the organisational structure and tenant environment.

**Submodules**

| Component             | Functionality                                 |
| --------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| Organisation profile  | Name, logo, registration and business details |
| Branch management     | Multiple offices, campuses and locations      |
| Department management | IT, HR, Finance, Operations, etc.             |
| Employee directory    | Staff profiles and contact information        |
| Teams                 | Groups of users and technicians               |
| Designations          | Job titles and organisational positions       |
| Reporting hierarchy   | Department heads and supervisors              |
| Workspace settings    | Organisation-specific configuration           |
| Custom fields         | Organisation-defined data fields              |
| Business calendar     | Working days, holidays and operating hours    |

**Special functionality**

- Multi-branch organisations.
- Separate branch-level administrators.
- Department-level access.
- Organisation-specific ticket numbering.
- Configurable currency and timezone.
- Custom approval hierarchies.
- Organisation branding.
- Organisation data export.

For service providers, the organisation structure additionally includes a client hierarchy.

A provider might have 30 clients, each with 3 branches and multiple departments.

All client data remains isolated.

# **5. Database architecture and schema**

This is one of the most important decisions in the project.

A weak database design will make multi-tenancy, reporting and cross-module workflows extremely difficult later.

I recommend a relational database with strict tenant isolation.

## **5.1 Database design principles**

1. Every tenant-owned business record must belong to an organisation.
2. Client records managed by service providers must also identify the owning provider.
3. Users must have explicit workspace memberships.
4. Every significant operational activity must be traceable.
5. Assets, tickets, projects and work orders should have relationships.
6. Financial records must preserve historical values.
7. Deletion should normally be soft deletion where business history must be retained.
8. Sensitive documents require separate file-access controls.
9. Reporting must not bypass tenant permissions.
10. Database transactions must protect multi-step financial and operational changes.

### **Multi-tenant ownership model**

There are two different ownership relationships:

- `tenant_id`: the organisation that owns the data.
- `provider_id`: the service provider responsible for managing the client, when applicable.

For an ordinary organisation, the tenant owns its records.

For an IT service provider, the provider owns the client relationship, but client data must remain logically and permissionally separated.

A recommended model is:

```
TECHPROS PLATFORM
       |
       +---- Service Provider Tenant
       |           |
       |           +---- Client A Tenant
       |           +---- Client B Tenant
       |           +---- Client C Tenant
       |
       +---- Independent Organisation Tenant
                   |
                   +---- Branches
                   +---- Departments
                   +---- Employees
                   +---- Assets
                   +---- Tickets
```

The client should have its own tenant boundary even though it is managed by another provider.

The provider receives explicitly granted delegated access rather than unrestricted access to all customer data.

## **5.4 Example foundational SQL schema**

The following illustrates how the core tenancy and ITSM records could be represented. It is a design starting point, not the complete migration set.

```

CREATE TABLE tenants (
    id TEXT PRIMARY KEY,
    name TEXT NOT NULL,
    slug TEXT NOT NULL UNIQUE,
    tenant_type TEXT NOT NULL
        CHECK (tenant_type IN (
            'organisation',
            'service_provider'
        )),
    status TEXT NOT NULL DEFAULT 'active',
    created_at TEXT NOT NULL DEFAULT CURRENT_TIMESTAMP,
    updated_at TEXT NOT NULL DEFAULT CURRENT_TIMESTAMP
);

CREATE TABLE users (
    id TEXT PRIMARY KEY,
    email TEXT NOT NULL UNIQUE,
    full_name TEXT NOT NULL,
    status TEXT NOT NULL DEFAULT 'active',
    created_at TEXT NOT NULL DEFAULT CURRENT_TIMESTAMP
);

CREATE TABLE tenant_memberships (
    id TEXT PRIMARY KEY,
    tenant_id TEXT NOT NULL,
    user_id TEXT NOT NULL,
    role_id TEXT NOT NULL,
    status TEXT NOT NULL DEFAULT 'active',
    created_at TEXT NOT NULL DEFAULT CURRENT_TIMESTAMP,

    FOREIGN KEY (tenant_id) REFERENCES tenants(id),
    FOREIGN KEY (user_id) REFERENCES users(id),

    UNIQUE (tenant_id, user_id)
);

CREATE TABLE branches (
    id TEXT PRIMARY KEY,
    tenant_id TEXT NOT NULL,
    name TEXT NOT NULL,
    address TEXT,
    status TEXT NOT NULL DEFAULT 'active',
    created_at TEXT NOT NULL DEFAULT CURRENT_TIMESTAMP,

    FOREIGN KEY (tenant_id) REFERENCES tenants(id)
);

CREATE TABLE assets (
    id TEXT PRIMARY KEY,
    tenant_id TEXT NOT NULL,
    branch_id TEXT,
    asset_tag TEXT NOT NULL,
    name TEXT NOT NULL,
    category TEXT NOT NULL,
    manufacturer TEXT,
    model TEXT,
    serial_number TEXT,
    status TEXT NOT NULL DEFAULT 'active',
    purchase_date TEXT,
    warranty_expiry TEXT,
    created_at TEXT NOT NULL DEFAULT CURRENT_TIMESTAMP,

    FOREIGN KEY (tenant_id) REFERENCES tenants(id),
    FOREIGN KEY (branch_id) REFERENCES branches(id),

    UNIQUE (tenant_id, asset_tag)
);

CREATE TABLE tickets (
    id TEXT PRIMARY KEY,
    tenant_id TEXT NOT NULL,
    branch_id TEXT,
    requester_id TEXT NOT NULL,
    assigned_user_id TEXT,
    asset_id TEXT,
    ticket_number TEXT NOT NULL,
    title TEXT NOT NULL,
    description TEXT,
    category TEXT,
    priority TEXT NOT NULL DEFAULT 'medium',
    status TEXT NOT NULL DEFAULT 'open',
    created_at TEXT NOT NULL DEFAULT CURRENT_TIMESTAMP,
    resolved_at TEXT,
    closed_at TEXT,

    FOREIGN KEY (tenant_id) REFERENCES tenants(id),
    FOREIGN KEY (requester_id) REFERENCES users(id),
    FOREIGN KEY (assigned_user_id) REFERENCES users(id),
    FOREIGN KEY (asset_id) REFERENCES assets(id),

    UNIQUE (tenant_id, ticket_number)
);

CREATE INDEX idx_assets_tenant
    ON assets(tenant_id);

CREATE INDEX idx_tickets_tenant_status
    ON tickets(tenant_id, status);

CREATE INDEX idx_tickets_assignee
    ON tickets(tenant_id, assigned_user_id, status);

```

Production implementation must additionally enforce tenant-consistent foreign-key relationships, membership validation, transaction boundaries, input validation and authorization at the application layer.

For financial and historical records, use immutable transaction references and preserve snapshots of relevant prices, tax settings and contractual terms.

## **5.5 Database technology decision**

My recommendation for the initial product is:

- Cloudflare D1 for the transactional database.
- Cloudflare R2 for file storage.
- Queues for asynchronous work.
- Workers for the API and application services.
- A reporting abstraction that can eventually support a separate analytical database.

D1 is appropriate for a carefully designed initial SaaS product, but we should not assume it is the ideal permanent home for every workload.

Because TechPros will eventually process complex reporting, monitoring metrics, financial transactions and cross-module relationships, the architecture must isolate the database access layer.

That gives us the ability to introduce PostgreSQL for more demanding workloads without rewriting the entire frontend or business logic.

For infrastructure metrics, I would avoid storing every raw measurement indefinitely in the transactional database. Use time-based aggregation, retention policies and a dedicated metrics storage strategy as monitoring grows.

The platform must implement:

1. Secure authentication.
2. Multi-factor authentication.
3. Session expiration and revocation.
4. Password reset protections.
5. Tenant isolation.
6. Server-side permission checks.
7. Rate limiting.
8. Audit logging.
9. File upload validation.
10. Encryption in transit and at rest.
11. Secure secret management.
12. Backup and recovery procedures.
13. Data export and deletion policies.
14. Administrative access reviews.

One especially important rule: never rely on frontend navigation hiding a feature as an access-control mechanism. Every operation must be authorised by the backend.

### **Important infrastructure separation**

Do not make the main API responsible for all background work.

For example:

- Generating 5,000-page reports should happen asynchronously.
- Sending bulk notifications should happen in a queue.
- Importing 20,000 assets should run as a background job.
- Monitoring agents should communicate through a dedicated ingestion service.
- AI requests should be handled by a controlled AI gateway.

This prevents heavy operations from affecting normal application responsiveness.

## **8.3 Authentication**

Authentication should support:

- Email and password.
- Email verification.
- Password recovery.
- MFA.
- Session management.
- Invitation-based registration.
- Organisation membership.
- Optional enterprise SSO in a later phase.

A managed identity provider can be used if it reduces development and security overhead, but identity and tenant membership must remain separate concepts.

## **8.4 File storage**

Use R2 for:

- Ticket attachments.
- Asset photographs.
- Purchase documents.
- Technical reports.
- Audit evidence.
- Network diagrams.
- Project deliverables.
- Generated PDFs.

Files should not be publicly accessible by default.

Use short-lived signed access or an authenticated download service. Store file metadata and tenant ownership in the database, not the file itself.

## **8.5 Monitoring and observability**

The platform itself must be observable.

Implement:

- API request logs.
- Error tracking.
- Performance metrics.
- Queue processing metrics.
- Failed job monitoring.
- Database health checks.
- Uptime monitoring.
- Security event monitoring.
- Audit event monitoring.

The platform's own infrastructure monitoring should be separate from customer network monitoring.

# **11. Quality assurance, security and operational reliability**

This is not a secondary consideration.

TechPros will potentially store information about business infrastructure, financial expenditure, equipment, internal processes and technical vulnerabilities.

A failure in security or data separation could damage customer trust significantly.

## **11.1 Security requirements**

| Area           | Requirement                                   |
| -------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| Authentication | Secure identity and session management        |
| Authorisation  | Server-side RBAC and resource permissions     |
| Tenancy        | Enforced organisation data isolation          |
| Encryption     | TLS and encrypted storage                     |
| Files          | Authenticated access and validation           |
| API            | Rate limits, validation and abuse protection  |
| Audit          | Traceable privileged and sensitive operations |
| Secrets        | Secure managed secrets                        |
| Database       | Backups, migrations and integrity checks      |
| Monitoring     | Security and operational alerts               |
| Recovery       | Tested restoration procedures                 |
| Privacy        | Data minimisation and retention controls      |

For the Nigerian market, the platform should be designed with the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 and relevant regulatory obligations in mind.

Enterprise customers may also request alignment with ISO 27001 and NIST security practices.

## **11.2 Testing strategy**

I would require these testing layers:

1. Unit tests for business logic.
2. Integration tests for database operations.
3. API tests for authentication and permissions.
4. Tenant isolation tests.
5. End-to-end tests for critical user journeys.
6. Load tests for important application endpoints.
7. File upload and download security tests.
8. Backup restoration tests.
9. Migration tests.
10. Browser and mobile responsiveness tests.

### **Non-negotiable test scenarios**

- User from Organisation A cannot access Organisation B records.
- Technician cannot access an unassigned client workspace.
- Employee cannot approve their own restricted purchase request.
- Expired subscriptions enforce the configured access policy.
- Ticket closure updates reporting correctly.
- SLA calculations respect holidays and working hours.
- Duplicate monitoring alerts do not create unlimited incidents.
- Deleted users cannot continue using revoked sessions.
- Generated reports do not expose restricted fields.
- File URLs cannot be reused to access another tenant's documents.

## **19. My final strategic recommendation**

There is something important about the opportunity here.

TechPros should not try to become another generic software company with a website listing IT services.

It should own a distinct product category:

**IT Operations Management for Businesses and Service Providers.**

I would build the company around three complementary capabilities:

**TechPros One — Manage**

Manage IT operations, assets, teams, projects and business technology.

**TechPros Monitor — Observe**

Monitor infrastructure, devices, services and operational health.

**TechPros Intelligence — Understand**

Transform technical and operational information into insights, reports and actionable recommendations.

These are three product pillars, not necessarily three separate applications. They should initially operate as connected modules in the TechPros One ecosystem.

The strongest competitive advantage will not be the number of modules. It will be how intelligently they work together.

A ticket connected to an asset, an asset connected to a maintenance record, maintenance connected to procurement, procurement connected to expenditure, and all of it represented in a management report.

That is the product I would aim to deliver.

## **20. Recommended next step: Convert this blueprint into the engineering specification**

Before writing production code, I recommend producing four detailed development documents:

1. **Product Requirements Document (PRD):** Every module, feature, requirement, workflow, validation rule and acceptance criterion.
2. **Database & API Specification:** Complete entity relationships, SQL migrations, API endpoints, request/response contracts and permission enforcement.
3. **UI/UX Specification:** Every screen, navigation structure, dashboard, component, responsive behaviour and interaction.
4. **Implementation Master Plan:** Development tasks, dependencies, milestones, testing strategy and deployment procedures.
